# Mahan's Carousel Voice

Write a carousel that sounds like the same person who narrates his reels — but
knows the reader is holding the phone, not listening. Same lowercase, same hard
numbers, same "someone finally did it." Different physics.

A carousel is not a reel with the audio removed. It earns its reach a different
way, and that difference decides every structural choice below.

## Why carousels behave differently (read this before drafting)

A reel sells once, in three seconds, to a viewer who is being *shown* it. A
carousel sells twice: the cover has to stop the scroll, and **slide 2 has to earn
the swipe**. If nobody swipes, nothing else on the post matters.

The currency is also different. Reels are paid in views. Carousels are paid in
**saves** — a save is a reader deciding "I will need this later," and it is the
strongest signal the post did real work. Instagram also re-serves a carousel to
people who scrolled past without engaging, so a carousel has a longer tail than a
reel but a lower ceiling. Set expectations accordingly: a carousel is unlikely to
out-reach a good reel, and that is not the point. The point is that it converts
attention into saved, re-openable value, and it lets one piece of research be
published twice.

**The single most important principle for this account:**

Mahan's highest-saving reels all make a claim a reel physically cannot deliver —
"754 skills across 26 domains," "18 specialized agents," "every free API in 50
categories," "2.1 billion free tokens." People save those reels as a bookmark for
a list they never actually get to see. **A carousel is that list.** So when
choosing what to put on the slides, ask: *what did the reel promise but never
show?* Show that. The carousel exists to pay off a debt the video left open.

This means a carousel whose slides only restate the reel is a wasted post. If the
slides don't contain something the video couldn't fit, reconsider the topic.

## What the data says (his account, 122 reels, May–Aug 2026)

Full numbers are in `references/performance-data.md` — read it when picking a
topic or justifying a choice to him. The short version:

- Scripts saying **"free" / "open source"** averaged **25,303 views** vs **6,743**
  without. Roughly 3.75×. This phrase is not filler; it is the load-bearing beam.
- **Personal / opinion openers averaged 2,369 views** across 14 posts. Near-dead.
  Don't build carousels out of his takes, his routine, or AI news commentary.
- **Highest save rates** were all "big pile of X" reference topics: cybersecurity
  skill pack 6.3%, YouTube agent team 5.8%, Manus clone 5.5%, fast scraper 5.5%,
  SEO agent team 5.2%, free-API list 5.0%.
- Two posts (Sonnet 5, Fable 5 model drama) pulled 27–28k views on **1.4–3.3%
  engagement and 52 / 72 saves**. High reach, no saved value. Do not carousel
  model drama — the format runs on exactly the thing those posts failed at.
- His top 8 posts carry **64% of all his views**. Mining a proven winner beats
  inventing a new topic.

Use this to *choose*, not just to *justify*. If a requested topic looks like the
dead categories above, say so plainly in one line and offer the nearest proven
adjacent topic — then write whatever he confirms. He would rather be told than
handed a polished post that won't land.

## Voice

Inherit every rule from `mahan-script-voice`: lowercase, short declarative lines,
one idea at a time, a hard number early, "someone finally did it" framing that
removes him as the seller, no emojis, no hashtags in the slides, no adjective
hype, and the surprising specific saved for last.

Three things change because the reader is reading, not listening:

1. **Tighter.** A spoken line can run 15 words. A slide line cannot. Headline
   lines cap around 8 words; body lines around 12. If a slide needs a paragraph,
   it's two slides.
2. **Scannable, not sequential.** A listener can't skip. A reader can. Every slide
   must make sense if it's the only one someone reads, because that happens.
3. **No spoken filler.** "you know," "and the other best part is," "i'm not
   kidding" work out loud and look like padding in type. Keep the enthusiasm in
   the *fact*, not the framing.

Keep the numbers spoken-accurate: "754 skills," not "754+." He rounds honestly and
never inflates — see Accuracy below.

## Structure

Default to **9 slides**. Go to 7 if the payload is thin, 11 if the list genuinely
has more units and each one earns its slide. Never pad to hit a number; a tight 7
outperforms a bloated 11.

| Slide | Job |
|---|---|
| 1 — Cover | Stop the scroll. The single most striking fact, nothing else. |
| 2 — The stake | Earn the swipe. Why this matters *to them*, or the size of the pile they're about to get. |
| 3 to n−2 — Payload | One unit per slide. The actual list. This is what gets saved. |
| n−1 — The surprising specific | The weirdest, most niche item. This is what gets commented. |
| n — CTA | One ask. Nothing else on the slide. |

**Cover.** It is a headline, not a sentence. Lead with the number where one
exists — his best covers are a number plus a noun plus a consequence. Keep the
top-right corner clear of text; the slide counter sits there. Formulas that match
his proven hooks:

- `754 claude skills for cybersecurity` / `26 domains. all free.`
- `the $18,400/yr saas stack — replaced free`
- `this scraper is 1,600x faster`
- `someone finally did it` + what it is

**Slide 2** is the one most people get wrong. It is not the first list item. It is
the reason to keep going: the cost of not knowing, or the scale of what's coming.
"most agencies pay $4,200 a year for this. all six of these are free." Then swipe.

**Payload slides** each carry one unit — one tool, one agent, one API category,
one step. Name it, say what it does in a line, and where it's honest and known,
attach the number that makes it real: what it replaces, what that costs, how many
stars it has. Concrete beats comprehensive.

**Final slide** carries exactly one ask, in his CTA voice: `comment CYBER and i'll
send you the full guide.` Never stack a follow ask on top of a comment ask. The
keyword should be short, uppercase, and on-topic — it is a reach mechanism, since
comments are a ranking signal, so it needs to be a word someone can type without
thinking.

## Design

**He already has a design system. Read `references/design-system.md` before
designing anything, and do not invent an alternative.** It was derived from his
own 429K post and refined across 36 designs; a fresh palette would throw that away.

The two-line version so you know what you're committing to: warm paper ground
`#FAF7F2`, ink `#2A2A24`, coral `#EA9678` as a **fill only** (small type takes
`#C9694A` — coral fails contrast at small sizes on this ground), Archivo +
Archivo Black + JetBrains Mono, 1080×1350, with a `mahan ai.` header row and an
`@mahanaicoach` footer row framing every slide.

The structural idea worth holding onto is his, and it is the whole reason this is
repeatable: **a cover is generated from a content record** — name, what it
replaces, its price, star count, licence, screenshot. Five cover templates (Swap,
Proof, Kill, Field, Counter) each consume that record differently. So don't start
from "what should this look like." Start from what facts you actually hold, then
pick the template those facts can fill. No star count means no Counter; no
screenshot means no Proof.

Body slides come from the eight inside-slide templates, which are lighter than the
covers on purpose. A step-by-step deck is IN-5 Timeline; a stack is IN-8 Tile Grid;
a before/after is IN-2 Split Compare.

Deliver as a **Claude Design canvas** — one `.dc.html` artboard per slide plus
`canvas.json` — so he can edit and export the slides himself rather than asking
for a re-render. Put the verified/unverified fact split in `canvas.json`
annotations, the way he does.

## Caption

Short. The slides carry the content; the caption exists to repeat the CTA and
carry hashtags — the one place hashtags are allowed, since they're metadata rather
than voice. Two lines plus tags:

```
[one line restating the payoff in his voice]
comment [KEYWORD] and i'll send you the full guide
#tag #tag #tag #tag
```

Match his existing tag habit: 3–5 tags, lowercase, specific to the topic and the
tool, not generic reach tags.

## Accuracy

This matters more in a carousel than a reel, because a slide is a screenshot
someone can quote back at him. Every number on a slide should be checkable.

- Never invent a figure, a star count, a price, or a feature. If a number isn't
  verified, use a scale word ("a full set of," "still trending") or cut the beat.
- Only say "free" or "open source" when the license actually permits it and it is
  genuinely self-hostable.
- Dollar figures for what a tool "replaces" must come from the vendor's real
  public pricing, and should say which plan and per what period. `$1,200/yr` with
  no basis is the kind of thing that gets corrected in the comments.
- If his framing of a topic looks inflated, write the accurate version and add one
  line beneath the output noting what you softened and why. He has a documented
  history of on-camera overstatement and would rather be corrected than defended.

## Workflow

1. Identify the topic. If it came from an existing reel, find what the reel
   promised but couldn't show — that's the payload.
2. Check it against the dead categories in the data section. Flag in one line if
   it looks weak, then proceed with what he confirms.
3. Build the **content record** for each unit before writing a word of copy:
   name · what it replaces · that thing's price · star count · licence ·
   screenshot. Check his Notion Resource Pages first (see below). A missing field
   is not a gap to write around — it closes off templates, so know what you hold.
   Where a fact is genuinely missing, ask one focused question rather than padding.
4. Choose the slide count from how many units genuinely earn a slide, and the
   cover template from what the record can fill.
5. Draft cover and slide 2 first. If those two don't work, the rest is wasted —
   rewrite them before continuing.
6. Fill the payload, put the weirdest item at n−1, close on one CTA.
7. Reread as a reader: cut every word that doesn't survive being read silently at
   arm's length.
8. Note any softened claim on one line at the end.

## Output format

Return the carousel as plain text, one block per slide, in this shape. No markdown
bold inside slide copy, no emojis, lowercase throughout except the CTA keyword and
proper nouns.

```
CAROUSEL: [short internal name]
topic: [what it is] · units: [n] · proven by: [which reel + its number]

--- slide 1 / cover ---
headline: [≤8 words]
sub: [≤12 words, optional]

--- slide 2 / stake ---
headline: [≤8 words]
body: [≤12 words]

--- slide 3 ---
headline: [the unit name]
body: [what it does · what it replaces · the number]

[...]

--- slide 8 / surprising specific ---
headline: [the weird one]
body: [why it's absurd that this exists for free]

--- slide 9 / cta ---
comment [KEYWORD] and i'll send you the full guide

caption:
[line]
comment [KEYWORD] and i'll send you the full guide
#tag #tag #tag

design: [only if it deviates from the default spec]
note: [only if a claim was softened — say what and why]
```

If asked for options, vary the cover and which unit leads — not the underlying
structure. The structure is what makes it feel like his account.
